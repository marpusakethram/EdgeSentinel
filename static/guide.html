The idea in one minute

Imagine three machines in a small factory. They keep sending messages to a nearby computer: "I'm here" and "my temperature is 45 degrees." EdgeSentinel plays both sides on your laptop. The machines are pretend, but the messages really travel through a local network connection.

The app saves each message, checks a few rules, and shows a warning when something looks wrong. You can click that warning to see the exact messages behind it.

Your first try

  1. Go back and click Start / return to normal. All three cards should say Sending updates.
  2. Click Make the press run hot. Its pretend temperature changes from 45 C to 95 C.
  3. An alert appears because the demo limit is 80 C. Click it.
  4. Click Explain this alert. Read what happened and what to check next.
  5. Find a label like event:12. That means saved message number 12. Open the source data to check it.
  6. Click Start / return to normal again. The press cools down and the old alert says Back to normal.

What the other buttons do

  • Make cooling go quiet: cooling-03 stops sending messages. After 6 to 7 seconds, the app notices. It cannot tell whether a real machine would be broken or disconnected.
  • Report failed connections: conveyor-02 sends three made-up reports of failed attempts. No outside connection is attempted.
  • Report an unapproved write: press-01 reports a request that does not match the allowed sender and operation. A "write" means a request to change a setting. This demo never carries out the change.
  • Clear demo and stop: removes saved demo messages and alerts, then stops the built-in machines. Click Start when you want to try again.

Only one mode runs at a time. New buttons change the mode, but they do not delete old alerts. Failed-connection and write alerts stay Saved for review until you clear the demo.

A few words, in normal English

Device
One of the pretend machines.
Heartbeat
A regular "I'm here" message.
Telemetry
Sensor readings, such as temperature and vibration.
Gateway
The program that receives and checks the messages.
Event
One saved message with its own number.
Alert
A warning that one of the demo rules was crossed.
Evidence
The messages or observations that support that warning.
Runbook
A short troubleshooting guide stored in this project.
127.0.0.1
Your own computer. Another person's computer has its own 127.0.0.1.

Is this AI? Is it fixing anything?

No model is running here. The helper reads saved messages, picks a matching guide, and puts together a short answer with source labels. It does not guess a physical cause, change a machine setting, or fix anything automatically.

The rules and numbers are for learning. An alert does not prove there is a real fault or attack. No real factory equipment is connected.
